---
name: Comic requirements
description: The user's essential expectations for this Kazakh traditions comic app.
---

The user needs actual illustrated, cartoon-style comics, not prompts or text scenarios alone. Stories show a child asking an adult relative about Kazakh traditions, with a Kazakh setting such as a киіз үй. Vary both the child and the adult: boys and girls with different names, speaking to a grandmother, grandfather, mother, father, aunt, or brother-in-law.

**Why:** The user repeated that script-only output was not the requested product. Their initial girl–grandmother example was later explicitly broadened: they do not want every story to use Айша and әже.

**How to apply:** Every successful comic-generation result must contain illustrations and dialogue. Do not silently replace a failed illustration with a text-only result. Keep the chosen pair consistent within a story, including artwork and speech labels.

Use this three-stage conversation: the child asks what the tradition is; asks what people do during it; then asks what it means or why it helps. The adult answers each question.

**Why:** The user explicitly specified this sequence rather than an arbitrary three-part story.

**How to apply:** Keep one child question and one adult explanation in each frame, in that order of topics.

Keep the overall presentation compact. Do not show a sample three-panel comic or reflection questions in the headline/hero area.

**Why:** After reviewing the illustrated hero, the user explicitly asked to remove its three pictures and questions and replace them with something else.

**How to apply:** Reserve illustrated panels and reflection questions for generated results. Keep the introduction simple, without oversized typography or spacing.

Keep three illustrated panels in one horizontal row on all screen sizes. Dialogue belongs inside the comic frames but must not obscure the illustrations. Below every comic, include “Дәстүрдің мәні” and a family reflection assignment: ask parents about the tradition, describe what happened (or why it was not held), and explain the child's understanding. Provide a place to write an answer.

**Why:** The user confirmed they like the three-panel approach, explicitly requested horizontal rather than vertical placement, and asked for these learning prompts, using тұсаукесер as an example.

**How to apply:** Use horizontal scrolling on narrow screens rather than stacking panels. Match the question to the comic's tradition rather than asking about тұсаукесер for every story. Do not restore the removed introductory sentence describing Айша learning through a three-panel comic.

Keep topic suggestions organized into four blocks with three traditions each: childhood, marriage/family formation, social relationships/mutual help, and national holidays/everyday customs.

**Why:** The user explicitly supplied this four-part grouping and asked to replace the flat topic list.

**How to apply:** Preserve the four-category, three-topic structure instead of adding arbitrary flat suggestions.

The suggested traditions are examples, not a limit. Support custom tradition names and prioritize short waits. Keep the child's response-writing area and a separate quick question-and-answer area titled “Түсінбегеніңді сұра”.

**Why:** After removing the “ИИ-ден сұра” interface, the user explicitly requested a question box again under a correctly phrased Kazakh title, where a child asks and receives a fast explanation.

**How to apply:** Do not restrict generation to the twelve buttons or claim uncertain cultural facts as established. Use the child-friendly heading “Түсінбегеніңді сұра” and a simple “Сұрау” button, keep the child's written reflection separate, and avoid promising literally instantaneous AI responses.

The requested theme/headline is “Қазақ халқының дәстүрлерін комикс арқылы насихаттау”.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested this wording.

**How to apply:** Preserve this theme when changing the app's copy.
